Vail, Beaver Creek and Breckenridge restaurants

Six recognized restaurants in three resort areas along Interstate 70: the long-established Vail Valley list, and Breckenridge's 2026 arrival in the MICHELIN Guide.

One corridor, three resort areas, six recognized restaurants

Most visitors to Colorado's ski country never leave Interstate 70. The highway climbs west from Denver, passes through the Eisenhower-Johnson Memorial Tunnels into Summit County, crosses Vail Pass and drops into the Vail Valley. Along that line sit three of the state's best-known resort areas: Breckenridge, Vail and Beaver Creek with its neighbor Avon. Between them they hold six MICHELIN-recognized restaurants after 2026, and the corridor is the most reachable part of the high country for a dinner-led trip.

These towns are close on a map but different at the table. Vail and Beaver Creek have been inside the guide since 2023, so their list is long-established and steady. Breckenridge only became eligible in 2026, and arrived with both restaurants of one of Colorado's most decorated chefs. None of the six holds a star; the high-country star remains in Aspen.

How these picks are ordered. Within each area we list restaurants in the order we would suggest them to a first-time visitor, starting with the one with the longest run in the guide or the strongest independent recognition. Read it as a suggested sequence, not a rating, and know that no entry is sponsored.

Vail, Beaver Creek and Avon

  1. Sweet Basil

    MICHELIN Recommended · 193 Gore Creek Dr., Vail Village

    Open in Vail Village since 1977, Sweet Basil has appeared in all four Colorado editions of the guide. Vail Village is a pedestrian core, so there is no parking at the door: plan to walk in from the village parking structures or your lodging. It is the restaurant most first-time visitors are pointed toward, and the guide's repeated listing suggests that reputation is earned.

    Official website

  2. Osaki's

    MICHELIN Recommended · 100 E. Meadow Dr., Vail

    A very small sushi restaurant: MICHELIN describes a handful of counter seats and a few tables, with the day's fish written on a whiteboard and served as nigiri or sashimi. The guide's listing notes that it does not take bookings through MICHELIN's site, so contact the restaurant directly. Small rooms like this fill quickly in ski season.

    MICHELIN Guide listing

  3. Splendido at the Chateau

    MICHELIN Recommended · 17 Chateau Lane, Beaver Creek

    The formal option in the valley, high on the hill at Beaver Creek and on the MICHELIN list since 2023. Choose it for a special-occasion dinner when you want a classic resort dining room rather than a counter.

    MICHELIN Guide listing

  4. Mirabelle

    MICHELIN Recommended · 55 Village Rd., Avon

    French cooking in an old farmhouse below Beaver Creek, minutes from Splendido yet far less formal. At 7,431 feet, Avon is the lowest town on our mountain register.

    MICHELIN Guide listing

Breckenridge, at 9,600 feet

Breckenridge is the highest town on Colorado's restaurant register and the newest name in the mountain section of the guide. Both of its entries belong to the same chef, which tells you how concentrated the recognition is.

  1. Rootstalk

    MICHELIN Recommended (new 2026) · 207 N. Main St., Breckenridge

    A Summit County native, Matt Vawter came back to the mountains after Denver kitchens including Fruition and Mercantile, and in 2020 turned an old house on Main Street into Rootstalk. The James Beard Foundation named him the Mountain region's best chef in 2024, and when MICHELIN could finally look at Breckenridge in 2026, it put Rootstalk on the Recommended list.

    MICHELIN Guide listing

  2. Radicato

    MICHELIN Recommended (new 2026) · Main St., Breckenridge

    Vawter's Italian follow-up dates from 2022 and takes its name from the Italian for deeply rooted. It entered the guide alongside Rootstalk, and its chef, Cameron Baker, collected one of Colorado's four 2026 MICHELIN special awards, for young chefs and culinary professionals.

    MICHELIN Guide listing

Cooking at 9,600 feet. Water boils near 194°F in Breckenridge, by interpolation from Colorado State University Extension figures, so every braise, stock and pot of pasta takes longer than a sea-level recipe expects. It is one reason a strong kitchen this high is an achievement. For diners, the practical advice is the same as anywhere in the mountains: hydrate, and do not make the biggest dinner of the trip your first night at altitude.

Planning the drive and the reservation together

The I-70 corridor is the most reachable part of the mountains and also the most affected by traffic. Westbound traffic out of Denver on winter Friday evenings and weekend mornings, and eastbound traffic on Sunday afternoons, can add a lot of time to the drive. A dinner reservation in Vail or Breckenridge is easier to keep if you arrive the day before or travel midweek.

  • Check the road before you go. The Colorado Department of Transportation posts conditions, closures and chain or traction-law alerts on COtrip.org. Mountain passes and the tunnel approaches can close temporarily for weather or crashes.
  • Build a buffer. If a dinner is the reason for the trip, give yourself slack. A ninety-minute drive in good conditions can take much longer in a storm.
  • Mind the shoulder seasons. Spring and late-autumn lulls see some kitchens take a break of a few weeks. Confirm with the restaurant directly.
  • Walk where you can. Vail Village and Breckenridge's Main Street are both walkable cores, and parking near them is limited. Leave the car at your lodging if you can.

If you are continuing west, Glenwood Springs is the turn for Aspen, and the Grand Valley beyond it is Colorado's orchard and wine country. Our road-trip guide covers the full I-70 drive, and the Aspen guide picks up from Glenwood.

Which town should you choose for dinner?

For the strongest independent credentials, Breckenridge: a James Beard Best Chef winner and a 2026 MICHELIN special award in two restaurants on the same street. For depth and the longest MICHELIN track record, Vail, where Sweet Basil and Osaki's have been listed every year since 2023. For a formal night out, Splendido in Beaver Creek, with Mirabelle in Avon as the more relaxed alternative a few minutes downhill.

Questions

Common questions

Does Vail have a MICHELIN star restaurant?

No. Vail's two MICHELIN-recognized restaurants, Sweet Basil and Osaki's, are both Recommended, and both have been listed in every Colorado selection since 2023. The only starred restaurant in Colorado's mountains is Bosq in Aspen.

Which Breckenridge restaurants are in the MICHELIN Guide?

Rootstalk and Radicato, both from chef Matt Vawter, joined the MICHELIN Recommended list in 2026, the first year Breckenridge was eligible. Radicato's Cameron Baker also won the 2026 MICHELIN Young Chef/Culinary Professional Award.

Who is Matt Vawter?

The chef-owner of Rootstalk and Radicato in Breckenridge. He grew up in Summit County, cooked at Fruition and Mercantile in Denver, opened Rootstalk in 2020 and won the James Beard Award for Best Chef: Mountain in 2024.

How do I check I-70 conditions before driving to dinner?

Use COtrip.org, run by the Colorado Department of Transportation, which posts closures, chain and traction-law alerts and travel times for I-70 and the mountain passes.
