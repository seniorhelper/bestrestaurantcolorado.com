Home / MICHELIN stars in Colorado
Short answer

Colorado has ten MICHELIN-starred restaurants after the 2026 MICHELIN Guide Colorado, announced on 14 September 2026: The Wolf's Tailor in Denver holds two stars, and nine hold one star. Seven of the nine are in Denver (Alma Fonda Fina, Beckon, Brutø, Kizaki, Margot, Mezcaleria Alma and the newly starred Milpero), plus Frasca Food and Wine in Boulder and Bosq in Aspen. Thirteen restaurants hold a Bib Gourmand and 40 are Recommended, for 63 in total, in the first selection to cover the whole state.

The 2026 change

For three years, most of Colorado could not win a star at any price.

What changed about the MICHELIN Guide in Colorado in 2026?

The geographic boundary was removed. From 2023 through 2025, inspectors only considered restaurants in Denver, Boulder, Aspen, Snowmass Village, the Town of Vail and Beaver Creek Resort. For 2026 MICHELIN expanded the selection to the entire state, and the first statewide list was announced on 14 September 2026.

The mechanism behind the old boundary explains a set of results that otherwise look inexplicable. When MICHELIN enters a new region, the areas covered are determined in part by which destination marketing organizations contribute toward the cost of establishing the guide there. Six Colorado areas did. The rest of the state did not, and its restaurants were never eligible: not overlooked, not judged and rejected, simply outside the map.

That left Caroline Glover's Annette in Aurora, the 2022 James Beard Best Chef: Mountain winner, and Matt Vawter's Rootstalk in Breckenridge, the 2024 winner, structurally excluded from Colorado's own guide.

The 2026 results answered both cases. Annette earned a Bib Gourmand and Rootstalk joined the Recommended list. The expansion also brought the first entries in Colorado Springs and Telluride, and Aurora gained a second listing in Dân Dã.

What it did not bring was a wave of new stars. Milpero in Denver, chef Johnny Curiel's third starred restaurant, was the only new star in the state, and no restaurant outside the old six areas was starred.

MICHELIN Green Star

A separate award, not a lesser one.

The Green Star recognizes restaurants held accountable for their environmental and ethical standards: sustainable suppliers, waste avoidance, and reducing plastic and other non-recyclable material in the supply chain. It sits alongside the star system rather than beneath it. Bib Gourmand

The category people misread.

Created in 1997, the Bib Gourmand marks restaurants offering a full meal at a reasonable price. The guide describes the cooking as simpler and more recognizable, not worse, and many chefs regard it as the harder award. Colorado has 13 after 2026, three of them new.

Questions

How the guide actually works.

The star system is widely misunderstood, particularly the difference between a star, a Bib Gourmand and a Green Star. These are the questions we get asked most. For the full statewide picture including James Beard recognition, see the main register; for the mountains, mountain town restaurants; and for the other major award, the James Beard Awards explained.

How many MICHELIN star restaurants are in Colorado?

Ten, after the 2026 selection. The Wolf's Tailor in Denver holds two stars, the only two-star restaurant in the state, and nine restaurants hold one star. Seven of the nine are in Denver, one is in Boulder and one is in Aspen. Colorado has never had a three-star restaurant.

Which Colorado restaurant has two MICHELIN stars?

The Wolf's Tailor, at 4058 Tejon Street in Denver's Sunnyside neighborhood. It earned its second star in the 2025 selection, becoming the first two-star restaurant in Colorado history, and kept both stars in 2026. It serves an eclectic multicourse tasting menu built around Colorado grain and local sourcing.

What was the only new MICHELIN star in Colorado in 2026?

Milpero in Denver, at 3455 Ringsby Court. It is chef Johnny Curiel's third starred restaurant, alongside Alma Fonda Fina and Mezcaleria Alma in LoHi. Every restaurant that held a star in 2025 kept it in 2026.

Does the MICHELIN Guide cover all of Colorado?

Yes, since the 2026 edition. Before 2026 the selection covered only Denver, Boulder, Aspen, Snowmass Village, the Town of Vail and Beaver Creek Resort. The first statewide selection, announced on 14 September 2026, added restaurants in Aurora, Breckenridge, Colorado Springs and Telluride.

Why were some Colorado cities excluded from the MICHELIN Guide before 2026?

Because of how the guide enters a new region. The original geographic boundaries were set according to which destination marketing organizations contributed to the cost of establishing the guide locally. Denver, Boulder, Aspen and Snowmass, Vail and Beaver Creek did. Colorado Springs, Fort Collins, Durango, Steamboat Springs and the rest did not, so their restaurants could not be considered until the 2026 statewide edition.

What is a MICHELIN Green Star?

A separate award for sustainability, not a quality tier. It recognizes restaurants that hold themselves accountable for their environmental and ethical standards, work with sustainable producers, avoid waste, and reduce plastic and other non-recyclable materials in their supply chain. In the 2025 selection four Colorado restaurants held one: The Wolf's Tailor and Brutø in Denver, and Blackbelly and Bramble & Hare in Boulder.

Is a Bib Gourmand better or worse than a MICHELIN star?

Neither. It measures a different thing. A star rates cooking; a Bib Gourmand rates a full meal at a reasonable price, in a style the guide describes as simpler and more recognizable rather than lower in standard. Colorado has thirteen after the 2026 selection, including first-time Bibs for Annette in Aurora and for Pig and Tiger and Rougarou in Denver.

What does MICHELIN Recommended mean?

That inspectors found the food above average, but not at star or Bib Gourmand level. It is the entry point into the guide and the largest category in Colorado, with forty restaurants in 2026. For a traveller it is often the most useful tier, because these are recognized restaurants you can realistically book on short notice.

How do MICHELIN inspectors judge a restaurant?

On five criteria: the quality of the ingredients, the harmony of the flavors, mastery of cooking techniques, the personality of the chef as expressed in the cuisine, and consistency both between visits and across the menu. Inspectors are anonymous and the selection is independent of the marketing arrangements the guide has with tourism bodies.

Can a restaurant pay for a MICHELIN star?

No. The selection is made by anonymous inspectors and MICHELIN states the process is independent, with destination marketing organizations involved in marketing and promotion only. What money influenced historically was which geographic areas inspectors visited at all, which the 2026 statewide expansion resolved.
