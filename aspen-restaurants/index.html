Aspen restaurants: the four the guide recognizes

Aspen holds more MICHELIN-recognized restaurants than any other Colorado mountain town, including the only star in the high country. Here is how to choose between them and when to go.

Updated 10 October 2026  /  By Zach Wennstedt  /  Editorial picks, criteria explained below

Four recognized restaurants in a town of a few thousand

Aspen sits at 7,908 feet at the top of the Roaring Fork Valley, and for a small town it carries an unusual weight in Colorado's restaurant guides. It holds four MICHELIN-recognized restaurants after the 2026 selection, more than any other mountain town, including the only star in the high country. Aspen has been inside the guide's Colorado boundary since the first edition in 2023, alongside neighboring Snowmass Village, so its list is also the most settled: all four of these restaurants were already listed before the guide went statewide.

What follows is the short list we would give a friend planning a first trip. It is built only from restaurants an independent body has recognized, it explains why each one is worth the reservation, and it links to the restaurant's own website where we could verify one. We do not list opening hours or prices, because both change with the season here more than almost anywhere else in the state.

How these picks are ordered. We start with the starred restaurant, then list the three Recommended restaurants in the order we would suggest them for a first visit: central location first, then wine depth, then daytime practicality. It reflects our judgement only; we never score restaurants, and nobody pays to be here.

  1. Bosq

    One MICHELIN Star · 312 S. Mill St., Aspen

    Chef-owner Barclay Dodge's restaurant is the only kitchen in the Colorado mountains with a MICHELIN star. It has held one since the first Colorado selection in 2023 and kept it in 2026. The cooking leans on what can be foraged or grown close by, worked through techniques Dodge borrows from Japanese, French and Mexican kitchens, so the menu reads as local rather than imported luxury. Sommelier Nick Heilman won MICHELIN's 2025 Sommelier Award, the only special award that year to go outside Denver.

    Book it for the one big dinner of a trip, and plan it for your second or third night once you have adjusted to the altitude.

    Official website

  2. Prospect

    MICHELIN Recommended · Hotel Jerome, 330 E. Main St.

    The dining room of the Hotel Jerome, now part of the Auberge Collection. MICHELIN's listing describes a three-course prix fixe it calls Taste of the Land and a longer Colorado Journey tasting menu, and the restaurant held a Wine Spectator Best of Award of Excellence for 2025. It is the most central of Aspen's recognized rooms, a short walk from the downtown core.

    Official website

  3. Element 47

    MICHELIN Recommended · The Little Nell, 675 E. Durant Ave.

    The restaurant at The Little Nell, at the base of Aspen Mountain. It was renamed from Montagna after a renovation in late 2012, and the name is a nod to silver, element 47 on the periodic table, and to the silver-mining boom that built the town. Colorado produce and meat anchor the menu, and the wine cellar has been reported at around 20,000 bottles, which makes it the place to go if the wine list is the point of the evening.

    Official website

  4. Mawa's Kitchen

    MICHELIN Recommended · Aspen Airport Business Center

    Chef-owner Mawa McQueen's restaurant sits out by the airport rather than in the downtown grid. The Aspen Chamber describes a hyper-seasonal menu and a business that also does catering, private-chef work and in-flight meals. It is also the most practical choice in this group for a daytime meal; check its own channels for which services are running when you visit.

    MICHELIN Guide listing

Timing a dinner around Aspen's seasons

Aspen runs on two peaks. Winter brings the ski crowd from roughly late November into early April, with the winter holidays and the Presidents' Day week the hardest periods of the year for reservations. Summer runs from late June through September and brings a different crowd, drawn by hiking, music and the town's summer cultural calendar. In both peaks the best tables go early: if a specific dinner matters, book it as soon as the restaurant's reservation window allows.

Between those peaks come the two shoulder periods locals call mud season, roughly mid-April to late May and again from mid-October into November. Some restaurants close for a few weeks so staff can rest after a long season; others shorten their hours. If you are traveling in those windows, check each restaurant's own website or call before you plan around it, and treat third-party listings with suspicion.

Altitude note. At Aspen's elevation water boils at roughly 198°F rather than 212°F, according to Colorado State University Extension figures, and the air is dry. Kitchens adjust for it; visitors should too. Drink water alongside the wine, go easy on the first night, and save the longest tasting menu for later in the trip.

Getting there

The year-round road route from Denver is I-70 west to Glenwood Springs, then Highway 82 up the Roaring Fork Valley. The shorter summer route over Independence Pass on Highway 82 closes for the winter, so check the Colorado Department of Transportation's COtrip site for its status before you plan on it. Aspen also has a regional airport, which is why Mawa's Kitchen's location near the airport business center is less remote than it sounds. Our road-trip dining guide covers the drive in more detail.

What about Snowmass?

Snowmass Village, a short drive from Aspen, is the quiet puzzle in Colorado's guide. It has been inside the MICHELIN boundary since 2023, named alongside Aspen in every selection, and it has never had a restaurant listed, including in 2026. That makes it the longest-running blank on our statewide register. It does not mean there is nowhere good to eat there; it means no Snowmass restaurant has yet been recognized by either of the two bodies we use. If you are staying in Snowmass, the four restaurants above are a short drive away.

How Aspen compares with the rest of the mountains

Aspen's advantage is concentration. Vail and Beaver Creek have four recognized restaurants between three resort areas, and Breckenridge has two, both from the same chef. Aspen packs four into one walkable town with an airport. If you want the single strongest mountain dinner in Colorado, the evidence still points to Bosq. If you want the deepest wine list in the mountains, Element 47 is the obvious candidate. For the I-70 resort towns, see our Vail, Beaver Creek and Breckenridge guide, and for the full list sorted by elevation, the mountain town register.

Questions

Common questions

Does Aspen have a MICHELIN star restaurant?

Yes. Bosq, at 312 S. Mill St., has held one MICHELIN star since the first Colorado selection in 2023 and kept it in the 2026 selection. It is the only starred restaurant in Colorado's mountain towns.

How many MICHELIN-recognized restaurants are in Aspen?

Four in the 2026 MICHELIN Guide Colorado: Bosq (one star), and Element 47, Prospect and Mawa's Kitchen (all Recommended).

When is mud season in Aspen?

Roughly mid-April to late May and again from mid-October into November, between the ski and summer seasons. Some restaurants close or shorten hours, so check directly with each restaurant before you travel in those weeks.

Is Independence Pass open in winter?

No. Highway 82 over Independence Pass closes for the winter season. The year-round route from Denver is I-70 to Glenwood Springs and then Highway 82 south. Check COtrip.org for current road status.
