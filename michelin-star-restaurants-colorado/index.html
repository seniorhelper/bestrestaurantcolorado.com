Short answer

Colorado has nine MICHELIN-starred restaurants: The Wolf's Tailor in Denver holds two stars, and eight hold one star — Alma Fonda Fina, Beckon, Brutø, Kizaki, Margot and Mezcaleria Alma in Denver, Frasca Food and Wine in Boulder, and Bosq in Aspen. Four restaurants hold a Green Star, ten hold a Bib Gourmand and thirty-one are Recommended, for fifty in total. The 2026 selection covers the entire state for the first time and has not yet been announced.

The 2026 change

For three years, most of Colorado could not win a star at any price.

What changed about the MICHELIN Guide in Colorado for 2026?

The geographic boundary was removed. From 2023 through 2025, inspectors only considered restaurants in Denver, Boulder, Aspen, Snowmass Village, the Town of Vail and Beaver Creek Resort. For the 2026 edition MICHELIN expanded the selection to cover the entire state, and its anonymous inspectors have been making reservations across Colorado since early in the year.

The mechanism behind the old boundary is worth understanding, because it explains a set of results that otherwise look inexplicable.

When MICHELIN enters a new region, the areas covered are determined in part by which destination marketing organisations contribute financially toward the marketing costs of establishing the guide there. Six Colorado areas did. The rest of the state did not, and its restaurants were therefore never eligible — not overlooked, not judged and rejected, simply outside the map.

The results were strange. Caroline Glover's Annette in Aurora won a James Beard Award for Best Chef: Mountain in 2022 and was structurally excluded from Colorado's own guide. Matt Vawter's Rootstalk in Breckenridge won the same award in 2024 and was likewise ineligible. Two of the strongest kitchens in the state could not be considered.

The international director of the guide framed the expansion around going beyond the main urban areas, and the Colorado Tourism Office framed it around inspiring culinary travellers to discover destinations in every corner of the state.

Practically, three things follow. The candidate pool roughly doubles. The towns most likely to gain are the ones nobody has been watching — coverage of the announcement specifically raised Cänon City, Durango, Grand Junction and Steamboat Springs. And search demand around Colorado dining will spike sharply for a few days and then settle into a new, higher baseline.

Colorado has now been a MICHELIN region since 2023, joining a list that includes New York, Chicago, California, Florida, Toronto, Vancouver, Atlanta, Mexico, Texas, Quebec, the American South, Philadelphia, Boston and most recently the Southwest.

Bib Gourmand

The category people misread.

Created in 1997, the Bib Gourmand marks restaurants offering a full meal at a reasonable price. The guide describes the cooking as simpler and more recognisable — not worse. Many chefs regard it as the harder award, because excellence has to arrive inside a price ceiling that starred kitchens never face. Colorado has ten, and reading them as runners-up is the most common mistake made with this guide.

Questions

How the guide actually works.

The star system is widely misunderstood, particularly the difference between a star, a Bib Gourmand and a Green Star. These are the questions we get asked most.

For the full statewide picture including James Beard recognition, see the main register. For the mountains specifically, see mountain town restaurants.

How many MICHELIN star restaurants are in Colorado?

Nine. The Wolf's Tailor in Denver holds two stars, the only two-star restaurant in the state, and eight restaurants hold one star. Six of the eight are in Denver, one is in Boulder and one is in Aspen. Colorado has never had a three-star restaurant.

Which Colorado restaurant has two MICHELIN stars?

The Wolf's Tailor, at 4058 Tejon Street in Denver's Sunnyside neighbourhood. It earned its second star in the 2025 selection, becoming the first two-star restaurant in Colorado history, and it also holds a MICHELIN Green Star for sustainability for the third consecutive year. It serves an eclectic multicourse tasting menu built around Colorado grain and local sourcing.

Does the MICHELIN Guide cover all of Colorado?

As of the 2026 edition, yes. Before 2026 the selection covered only Denver, Boulder, Aspen, Snowmass Village, the Town of Vail and Beaver Creek Resort. MICHELIN announced in February 2026 that inspectors would consider restaurants across the entire state, and they have been in the field since. The full 2026 list has not yet been published.

When does the 2026 MICHELIN Guide Colorado come out?

MICHELIN has not announced a date. Its February 2026 release said only that the full selection would be revealed later in the year. The 2025 Colorado selection was announced on 15 September 2025, and the guide has typically been released in early September, so that window is the reasonable expectation. We will update this page when the list is published.

Why were some Colorado cities excluded from the MICHELIN Guide?

Because of how the guide enters a new region. The geographic boundaries were set according to which destination marketing organisations contributed financially to the marketing costs of establishing the guide locally. Denver, Boulder, Aspen and Snowmass, Vail and Beaver Creek did. Colorado Springs, Fort Collins, Durango, Steamboat Springs and the rest did not, so their restaurants were never eligible regardless of quality.

What is a MICHELIN Green Star?

A separate award for sustainability, not a quality tier. It recognises restaurants that hold themselves accountable for their environmental and ethical standards, work with sustainable producers, avoid waste, and reduce or remove plastic and other non-recyclable materials from their supply chain. Four Colorado restaurants hold one: The Wolf's Tailor and Brutø in Denver, and Blackbelly and Bramble & Hare in Boulder. Two of those four hold a Green Star without holding a star.

Is a Bib Gourmand better or worse than a MICHELIN star?

Neither. It measures a different thing. A star rates cooking; a Bib Gourmand rates a full meal at a reasonable price, in a style the guide describes as simpler and more recognisable rather than lower in standard. Many chefs consider a Bib harder to earn, because the quality has to be delivered inside a price constraint that starred restaurants do not face. Colorado has ten.

What does MICHELIN Recommended mean?

That inspectors visited and found the food above average, but not at star or Bib Gourmand level. It is the entry point into the guide and the largest category in Colorado with thirty-one restaurants. For a traveller it is often the most useful tier, because these are the recognised restaurants you can realistically get a table at on short notice.

How do MICHELIN inspectors judge a restaurant?

On five criteria: the quality of the ingredients, the harmony of the flavours, mastery of cooking techniques, the personality of the chef as expressed in the cuisine, and consistency both between visits and across the menu. Inspectors are anonymous, visit several times a year, and the selection process is independent of the marketing arrangements the guide has with tourism bodies.

Can a restaurant pay for a MICHELIN star?

No. The star selection is made by anonymous inspectors and MICHELIN states the process is completely independent, with destination marketing organisations involved in marketing and promotional activity only. What money influenced historically was which geographic areas inspectors visited at all, which is a different problem and the one the 2026 statewide expansion resolves.
