Colorado food road trip planner

Pick a drive and the number of nights. Get every recognized restaurant on the way in drive order, an anchor dinner for each night, and the elevation you will sleep at.

Updated 10 October 2026  /  By Zach Wennstedt  /  Built from the statewide register

Short answer

The drive with the most recognized stops outside the Front Range is I-70 west: Breckenridge, Vail, Avon and Beaver Creek, then down to Bin 707 Foodbar in Grand Junction, best over two or three days. For one starred dinner in the mountains, drive to Aspen for Bosq. The planner below lays out all five routes.

      How the planner builds a trip

      Every stop comes from the statewide register, so a restaurant only appears if the 2026 MICHELIN Guide Colorado or the James Beard Foundation has recognized it. The planner does not add anything on its own, and nobody pays to be suggested.

      Routes and drive order

      The five drives are the ones in our road-trip dining guide. Towns appear in the order you reach them heading out from Denver, with short detours, such as Breckenridge off I-70 at Frisco or Parker east of I-25, marked as detours so you can drop them. Towns along the way with no recognized restaurant, such as Glenwood Springs, Palisade and Pueblo, stay on the list because they are where you will want to stop for fuel, a soak or a fruit stand.

      Anchor dinners

      For each night, the planner picks one overnight town and one anchor dinner there. With "Most decorated" selected it prefers stars, then Bib Gourmands, then Recommended restaurants and James Beard picks. With the value setting it prefers Bib Gourmands and James Beard-recognized kitchens, which are usually easier to book and lighter on the bill. The last night always lands at the far end of the route, so a two-night I-70 trip ends in the Grand Valley rather than doubling back.

      Elevation

      The strip above the stop list shows the town elevations from the register, from Denver's 5,280 feet to Breckenridge's 9,600. If you are coming from sea level, the planner's sleeping elevation is worth a look: headaches and poor sleep are common on the first night above 8,000 feet, and alcohol hits harder. Keep the first dinner simple and save the long tasting menu for later.

      What it leaves to you

      It does not know opening days, seasonal closures or whether there is a table. Mountain kitchens often take a break in late April and early November, and Independence Pass on Highway 82 closes for the winter. Check COtrip.org for roads and each restaurant's own site for hours, then book the anchor dinners first. Denver itself has its own guide: Best Restaurant Denver covers the city neighborhood by neighborhood.

      Planning a trip for a business or event rather than dinner? Our sister site COBusinessList.com is a directory of Colorado businesses.

      Common questions

      How does the Colorado road trip planner work?

      Choose one of five drives from Denver and how many nights you have. The planner lists every MICHELIN or James Beard-recognized restaurant on that route in the order you reach it, then suggests one anchor dinner per night, favouring the most decorated kitchen in each overnight town.

      Does the planner give driving times?

      Only the rough figures we can source: about four hours from Denver to Grand Junction on I-70 in good conditions, and about an hour from Denver to Colorado Springs. Check COtrip.org from the Colorado Department of Transportation for live travel times and closures.

      Why are towns like Durango and Steamboat missing?

      The planner only uses restaurants with MICHELIN or James Beard recognition. As of the 2026 selection neither body had named a restaurant in Durango, Steamboat Springs, Crested Butte, Salida or Fort Collins.

      How accurate is the map?

      Pins mark towns, not restaurant buildings. They come from the town positions on our statewide plot. Each restaurant’s own website or guide listing has its exact address.
